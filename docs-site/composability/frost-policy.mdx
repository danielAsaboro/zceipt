title: "FROST policy"
description: "A threshold treasury makes receipts like any wallet, and proves they were authorized under its published t-of-n key."
---

A treasury whose spend key is split with FROST, 2-of-3 for instance, makes receipts like any
wallet. Any two signers sign with the receipt's own randomizer; the signature is an ordinary
`SpendAuthSig`, and the receipt doesn't show which two signed.

<Note>
Tested with the Zcash Foundation's re-randomized FROST over Pallas (`reddsa` 0.5.2) in a unit test
and conformance vectors, and on the Zcash public testnet: a 2-of-3 treasury (a testnet wallet's
spend key split by a trusted dealer, group key equal to its `ak`) signed a receipt for a real
payment, and `receipts verify` accepts it against the chain with its policy proof.
</Note>

## Policy proof

Plain threshold receipts prove spend authority. A **policy proof** (`zip311p1`) adds that the
receipt was authorized under the treasury's *published* key.

The holders publish the verifiable-secret-sharing commitment `C_0..C_{t−1}` and the number of
holders `n`. `C_0` is the key's `ak`. A receipt signed by any `t` carries `rk = C_0 + [alpha]G`
for each spend. The proof is a Schnorr proof of knowledge of each spend's `alpha` for
`rk − C_0`, bound to the receipt's digest, the commitment and a verifier's challenge.

The verifier learns "this payment was authorized under that t-of-n key".

```bash
receipts verify @receipt.txt --policy-commitment commitment.txt
```

`receipt.txt` holds the receipt and the policy proof (`zip311p1…`); `commitment.txt` lists the
commitment the treasury published, one point per line. Pin it.

## What the commitment shows

The key is shared with a polynomial of degree `t − 1`, so any `t` of `n` holders, and no fewer,
can sign; each holder checks its share against the commitment.

It does not show the dealer discarded the whole key. With a trusted dealer that is a trust
assumption; distributed key generation removes it.

The proof binds the receipt's key to `C_0` and nothing else. Whoever holds the whole key can prove
the same receipt under a commitment of any threshold with the same `C_0`. So the verifier takes
the commitment from the treasury's own publication and passes it with `--policy-commitment`;
without that flag `verify` says the commitment is the proof's own.
