---
title: "Same payer"
description: "Prove several payments were all signed with one spend key, without revealing it."
---

A DAO, grant program or payroll shows that **one treasury** paid everyone, with one link.

## Use

A batch is several receipts in one link. Pass more than one `--txid`:

```bash
zceipt create -w <wallet> -i <identity> --txid <txid1> --txid <txid2> --txid <txid3>
```

The link carries a same-payer proof (`k=zip311k1…`). Verifying the batch checks it:

```bash
zceipt verify @batch-same-payer-link.txt
```

A real three-payment testnet batch, total 0.022 ZEC, is at `examples/testnet/batch.txt`, and one
with its same-payer proof is `examples/testnet/batch-same-payer.txt`.

## How it works

Every receipt's spend signs under `rk_i = ak + [alpha_i]G`. If all payments came from one key,
then `rk_i − rk_0 = [alpha_i − alpha_0]G` for every `i`. The proof is a Schnorr proof of knowledge
of each difference, made non-interactive by hashing the whole statement (every `rk`, the
receipts' digests, the message) with the commitments.

- **Sound:** a valid proof means the prover knows every `alpha_i − alpha_0`. For different keys
  `x_i`, `x_0` that difference is `x_i − x_0`, and knowing it without both secrets would solve a
  discrete log.
- **Zero knowledge:** `(R_i, s_i)` can be simulated from `rk_i − rk_0` alone, so it reveals the
  relation it states and nothing about `ak`.
- **Bound:** the proof signs the receipts' digests in order. It can't be moved to other receipts,
  reordered or bent.

No circuit is involved; it is Schnorr proofs over the Pallas curve.

## Limits

- It shows one *signing key* made the payments, or keys whose relation the maker knows. It
  doesn't say which wallet, or who holds it.
- Two wallets that pool their keys can produce one.
- Payments made through [sibling accounts](/composability/sibling-accounts) have different
  keys; this proof does not link them.
