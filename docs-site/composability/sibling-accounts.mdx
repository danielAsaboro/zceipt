---
title: "Sibling accounts"
description: "Prove two payer tags come from accounts of one wallet, without saying which wallet or accounts."
---

<Warning>
This proof uses a **new Halo 2 circuit** with a **ported BLAKE2b gadget**. The gadget's authors
say theirs is unaudited, and the port is too. It has run on the Zcash public testnet (a proof over
the payer tags of real payments by account 0 and account 1 of one wallet) and on regtest. See
[forked circuits](/reference/forked-circuits).
</Warning>

[Payer tags](/composability/distinct-payers) tell wallets apart by account. A donor with two
accounts of one wallet gets two tags and counts twice. A sibling-account proof lets the donor
show the two are one wallet, and the verifier then counts them once. It is the donor's choice to
show it: a verifier can merge tags a payer discloses, never find the ones a payer hides.

## Use

```bash
receipts siblings -w <wallet> -i <identity> --accounts 0,1 --domain round-2026 --out s.txt
receipts verify @s.txt --domain round-2026
receipts verify @income.txt --payer-tag @t0.txt --payer-tag @t1.txt --siblings @s.txt --domain round-2026
```

The last line counts the two payer tags as one wallet. On the testnet run, three tags (accounts 0
and 1 of one wallet, and a second wallet) count as three without the proof and two with it.

The output lists the two tags the proof is about. With payer tags from real payments (each tag
tied to its payment by the payer-tag proof), a verifier learns the two payments came from accounts
of one wallet.

## How it works

Orchard account keys are hardened ZIP 32 children of the wallet's coin-level key:

```text
I  = BLAKE2b-512("Zcash_ExpandSeed", c_par || 0x81 || sk_par || i)
sk = I[..32]
nk = ToBase(BLAKE2b-512("Zcash_ExpandSeed", sk || 0x07))
tag = Poseidon(nk, domain)
```

The circuit takes private `(sk_par, c_par)` and indices `i_1`, `i_2`, computes both children's
`nk` with BLAKE2b, hashes them to tags, and checks them against public `tag_1`, `tag_2` and
`domain`. The parent key, the indices and the `nk`s stay private.

So a tag can't be claimed for another wallet's account without a BLAKE2b or Poseidon collision.

Suppose the BLAKE2b gadget were unsound anyway. The proof can only be used to merge tags, so what
a forger could show is that two tags *they* know the keys for are siblings, which makes their own
two wallets count once. Merging someone else's tag needs that tag's `nk`, which the forger would
have to find by inverting Poseidon. An unsound gadget would not let anyone be counted more times,
or hide a payment.
A test (`against_orchard::accounts_are_children_of_the_coin_key`) checks that the in-circuit
derivation equals the `orchard` crate's.

## Cost

BLAKE2b runs inside the circuit: four compressions, so the circuit is `k = 17` (the gadget's
16-bit XOR table). Proving takes about 72 seconds on the development machine, far larger than
an Action proof. The parameters are cached at `~/.cache/receipts/kce-params-17.bin` and checked
by digest.

## Limits

- Hardened derivation only, so it covers accounts of one seed, not independently generated
  wallets.
- Unaudited gadget and port.

Code: `crates/receipts-kce`. The testnet and regtest end-to-end runs tie a sibling proof to real payer tags. The proof's `coin_type` is not authenticated by the circuit, which is harmless because a tag is only used next to payer tags checked against a chain.
