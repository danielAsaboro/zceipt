---
title: "How to prove a payment from your wallet"
description: "Make a receipt in the browser with your seed phrase or viewing key. No terminal."
---

This guide makes a receipt for one payment you sent, on the page at
[zceipt.xyz/verify/make](https://zceipt.xyz/verify/make). Everything runs in the
browser tab: the key is held in memory on your device and is never sent.

You need your wallet's seed phrase, or the viewing key it exports (`uview1…` on mainnet,
`uviewtest1…` on testnet), and roughly the block your wallet was created at. Wallets show the
birthday with the seed backup or in their settings, and the ones that export a viewing key do so
from the same place.

Before you open the wallet, know what the scan does. The page reads the chain from the birthday to
the tip through the chain server it names above the button; that server sees your IP address, the
time and the block ranges asked for (from a page on zceipt.xyz), not the key, the notes or the
payment. In a browser a scan runs at about a minute per few thousand blocks: a wallet created last
week takes a few minutes, one created months ago takes much longer, and the page stops scanning
as soon as you tap a payment.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your wallet">
    Paste the seed phrase or viewing key into **Seed phrase or viewing key**.

    In **Wallet birthday (block)**, enter the height your wallet shows with its seed backup or
    in its settings. If you don't know it, any block before the wallet first received funds
    works; an earlier block only means a longer scan. A seed phrase also shows **Network**:
    pick Testnet or Mainnet.

    Press **Open wallet**. The page reads the chain from that block, showing
    *Reading your wallet: block … of …*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the payment">
    Your payments appear under **Which payment?** as they are found. Tap the one you want to
    prove.

    If the list stays empty, the page says *No payments from this wallet up to block …*: the
    birthday is after your first deposit. Go back and enter an earlier block.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a message">
    Type an invoice or order number in **Message**, such as `Order 1093`. The receipt signs it,
    so the merchant can match it to the order. Leave it empty if there is none.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the receipt">
    Press **Make receipt**. The page lists the outputs the receipt will reveal (recipient, amount,
    memo); untick any you want to keep sealed. It then fetches the payment's block and makes the zero-knowledge
    proof, which takes about half a minute. When it finishes you see
    *Proofs made for … Your viewing key stayed on this device.*
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign it">
    With a seed phrase, the receipt is signed straight away and opens on the verifier, ready to
    share.

    With a viewing key, the page asks for the seed phrase under **Sign it**:

    - To sign here, type the phrase and press **Sign**.
    - To sign on another device (a hardware signer or an offline machine), press
      **Sign elsewhere**. The page saves the unsigned receipt; sign it there with
      `zceipt sign r.txt --seed-stdin`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share it">
    On the verifier, use **Copy link**, **Save as file** or **Print or save as PDF**. The receipt
    travels in the link's `#fragment`, which browsers never send to a server. The verifier removes
    it from the address bar as soon as it has read it, because the address bar is what browser
    history and history sync (Chrome Sync, iCloud, Firefox Sync) keep; the copy and save buttons
    work from the receipt in memory.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the merchant sees

The recipient, the amount and the memo of the outputs you sent to others, your message, and
proof that the key which spent the money signed it. Not your balance, addresses or other
payments. See [what a receipt reveals](/concepts/what-a-receipt-reveals).

To check a receipt you were sent, see [how to check a receipt](/use/check). For batches,
claims and the other relationship proofs, use the [terminal](/use/terminal).
